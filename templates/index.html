The problem

Manual slide reading is slow and subjective

Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer in women worldwide, with about 2.3 million new cases a year (WHO). Diagnosis depends on a pathologist examining stained biopsy tissue under a microscope. That takes time and expertise, and different pathologists can disagree on the same slide.

Limited specialists

Trained pathologists are scarce, especially in rural and resource-constrained hospitals.

Fatigue and volume

One slide can hold thousands of fields of view. Reviewing many slides a day raises the chance of missed regions.

Hand-crafted features fall short

Older CAD systems relied on manual features (GLCM, LBP, HOG) that miss complex tissue patterns. CNNs learn features directly from pixels.

Our solution

End-to-end pipeline

  1. UploadBiopsy image or tissue patch (PNG/JPG/TIFF)
  2. PreprocessResize, crop, normalise pixels to [0, 1]
  3. CNN features3 conv blocks learn edges → nuclei → tissue architecture
  4. ClassifyDense layers + sigmoid give P(malignant)
  5. ExplainGrad-CAM heatmap or tile-level probability map
  6. ReportSaved to history with a printable report
Objectives

What this project set out to do

  • Study existing ML and deep-learning approaches for breast cancer classification
  • Preprocess histopathology data with augmentation
  • Design and train a CNN for binary classification (benign vs malignant)
  • Evaluate with accuracy, precision, recall, F1, confusion matrix and ROC-AUC
  • Build a web interface for upload and real-time prediction
  • Add explainability with Grad-CAM
Scope

In scope / out of scope

In: CNN model, preprocessing, training and evaluation, web interface, visualisation of metrics.

Out: Clinical deployment, hospital system integration, live slide scanning, multi-centre validation, regulatory approval.
